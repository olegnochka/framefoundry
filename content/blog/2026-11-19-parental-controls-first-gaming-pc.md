Short answer: on a kid's gaming PC, parental controls live in layers, and you need each layer that your child actually uses. Start with Windows itself through Microsoft Family Safety, then set up each game store and big game separately - Steam, the Epic Games launcher (which covers Fortnite), and Roblox each have their own controls, and none of them reads the settings of the others. Below is the checklist, with what each tool can and cannot do according to its maker's own help pages.

## Before you start

- **Give your child their own accounts.** The controls below are set on your child's own account in each service. If your child plays on your account, they have nothing to apply to.
- **Keep the PINs somewhere your child will not find them.** Epic's controls use a Parental Controls PIN, and Roblox's Parent mode asks you to create a 6-digit PIN.

## 1. Windows: Microsoft Family Safety

This is the base layer, because it covers the whole PC rather than one game. According to Microsoft's Family Safety page, it offers:

- **Screen time limits** for the whole device on Windows and Xbox, plus separate limits for individual apps and games on Windows, Xbox and Android.
- **Screen time requests** - when time runs out, your child can ask for more and you decide.
- **Content filters** that block apps and games you have not approved, and web and search filters that keep browsing to kid-friendly sites.
- **Activity summaries**, so you can see what was used and for how long.

One detail that surprises people: Microsoft says the web filters work in Microsoft Edge, and other commonly used browsers are blocked so the filter cannot be bypassed. If your child is used to Chrome, expect a question about it. Screen time limits can be managed from the Family Safety app on your own Android phone or iPhone.

## 2. Steam: Steam Families

Steam's old Family View has been replaced by Steam Families. Per Valve's Steam Families guide:

- A family can have up to 6 members, each added as an **adult** or a **child**. Only children are subject to parental controls, so add your child's account as a child.
- An adult sets the controls under **Account Settings > Family Management > Manage**, expands the child's entry and turns on **Enable Parental controls**.
- You can choose which games the child can play, restrict the Steam Store, community and chat, set playtime limits and see playtime reports.
- Children can send you a **purchase request** for their cart, which you approve and pay for from your phone or by email.

## 3. Epic Games and Fortnite

Epic's parental controls cover Fortnite, Rocket League, Fall Guys and the Epic Games Store. You set them by signing in to your child's account in the Epic Account Portal, or from Fortnite's own settings. According to Epic's parental controls page, you can:

- **Limit voice and text chat** to everybody, friends and teammates, friends only, or nobody. For children under 10, Epic caps chat at "Friends Only" regardless.
- **Require your PIN for friend requests** and for real-money purchases made with Epic payment.
- **Block paid random items**, so V-Bucks cannot be spent on items with a random reward.
- **Set an age rating** for Fortnite experiences and for new games in the Epic Games Store; anything above it needs your PIN.
- **Set daily time limits** for Fortnite, or time windows, with a warning 30 minutes before time is up. These follow the account across devices.

Two limits worth knowing: the PIN for Epic payments does not cover purchases made with V-Bucks, and it does not apply to payments made through other platforms such as Steam.

## 4. Roblox

Roblox gives you two routes, per its Parental Controls FAQ. You can **link your own Roblox account** to your child's, which lets you manage everything from your own phone and get emails such as spending notifications. Creating a linked parent account asks you to verify that you are 18 or older, by facial age estimation, a government ID or a credit card. Or you can use **Parent mode** on your child's device without an account of your own - Roblox says this is still rolling out and may not be available to everyone yet.

Once linked, you can manage:

- Daily screen time limits
- Content maturity and a list of allowed or blocked games
- Who can chat with your child, and their friends list
- Monthly spending limits, spend notifications and the Robux balance

Roblox notes that some settings move from parent to child as kids get older, so it is worth checking again around each birthday.

## The checklist

| Layer | Where | Set first |
|---|---|---|
| Windows | Microsoft Family Safety | Child account, device screen time, app and web filters |
| Steam | Steam Families | Add as child, game access, playtime limits, purchase requests |
| Epic and Fortnite | Epic Account Portal | PIN, chat level, age rating, time limits |
| Roblox | Settings > Parental Controls | Link parent account, screen time, chat, spending limit |

Then sit down with your child for five minutes and show them how to ask for more time or a new game. The request buttons in each tool work much better when everyone knows they are there.
