Short answer: between October 14, when we started logging part prices every morning, and October 31, 13 of the 17 parts in our builds got more expensive, 3 held steady and 1 got cheaper. The graphics cards moved the most. Memory, SSDs (solid state drives), the cheaper case and cooler and the Windows license each crept up a few dollars. Processors barely moved. Below is the part-by-part list, the one-day swings the month-end numbers hide, and what it did to the builds on our [builds page](builds.html). The full table and the raw log, a public CSV (comma-separated values) file, are on the [October price report](price-report.html).

## How the log works

Every morning we look up each part's lowest in-stock price on PCPartPicker, or Newegg's own price where Newegg sells the part directly. These are national US online prices, the prices we pay when we buy the parts for a PC. When a part moves more than 5% from the price on our builds page, the builds that use it are re-priced the same day.

One thing to know when reading the log: when a part sells out everywhere we trust, we swap in an equivalent, and the log follows the part in the build, so the two ends of a line can be different models. The table says where that happened.

Three things stand out.

**Graphics cards moved the most, mostly because of stock.** The Sapphire RX 9060 XT was $519.99 on October 14 and again on October 27. In between, and after, it kept selling out at the stores we buy from, and the lowest in-stock price on those days was $599.99 (October 25), $649.99 (October 30) and $729.99 (November 3). The RX 9070 XT line jumped from $829.99 to $1,079.99 overnight, October 23 to 24, when an Amazon offer on the Asus PRIME OC ended; we ended the month on a PowerColor Reaper at $919.99 instead. The RTX 5070 Ti changed model four times between October 24 and 30 as listings sold out or a cheaper equivalent appeared, and on November 3 the PNY OC was out of stock at Newegg and B&H, leaving Best Buy at $1,500.00. Our [fall price post](blog-graphics-card-and-memory-prices-fall-2026.html) covered why the averages are up; October showed the other half: a specific card's price depends on who has it in stock that morning.

**Memory and storage drifted up quietly.** The 16GB kit up $10, the 32GB kit up $20, the two SSDs up $9 and $20. The 32GB Corsair kit did spike to $567.99 on October 26, when Newegg's was the only listing on PCPartPicker, and fell back to $499.99 the next day; the Performance build went up $100 for that one day and came back down.

**The one part that got cheaper was our own mistake, corrected.** The power supply shows $139.99 from October 16 to 20. That was the white Snow Edition, an EU listing, not the black US unit in our builds. We caught it on October 21 and corrected the log to the right model at $75.90, where it has stayed. The wrong days are still in the file.

| Build | Oct 16 | Lowest in October | Highest in October | Nov 3 |

| Mainstream | $1,999 | $1,999 | $2,199 | $2,199 |
| Performance | $2,699 | $2,599 | $2,899 | $2,699 |

The Mainstream build is up $200, almost all of it the RX 9060 XT. The Quiet Creator is up $300: the RTX 5070 Ti, plus the Ryzen 7 9800X3D, flat all October, then $469.00 on November 1 and $479.00 on November 3 at Newegg. The Performance build ends where it started, though most of its parts cost a little more, because two swaps on October 30 pulled it back from its $2,899 high: the MSI board at $139.99 instead of the Asus, which had jumped to $211.25, and the Reaper instead of a Sapphire PULSE at $1,049.99.

## What this means if you are buying

- **A quote is good for 72 hours.** After that, our [terms](terms.html) say we re-price before taking a deposit, because a part can move 10% or more overnight.
- **Once you pay, your price is locked.** If a part rises before we buy it, we absorb it. If a part goes out of stock or its price changes before we can buy it, we contact you with options: wait, a named alternative with its price, or cancel.
- **Already have a decent PC?** With memory and graphics card prices this high, upgrading the right parts often beats buying a new one - see [upgrades](services.html).
