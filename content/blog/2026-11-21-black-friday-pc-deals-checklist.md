Short answer: a Black Friday deal on a gaming PC or a part is only a deal if five things check out - the exact model number, the price it actually sold for in recent weeks (not the "was" price), who is selling it, the return window, and who handles the warranty. Black Friday is Nov 27 this year, so there is still time to do the homework now and buy with a clear head on the day. Here is what to look at, with examples from our own daily parts price log.

## 1. The exact model number, not the name

Product names get reused. Two items with the same name on the box can differ in region, color, cooler, cables or warranty, and deal listings tend to show the short name.

We learned this in our own [price log](price-report.html). From Oct 16 to Oct 20 it tracked a "Toughpower GF1 850W" power supply at $139.99. That listing turned out to be the white Snow Edition, EU version. The black US unit we put in our builds, part number PS-TPD-0850FNFAGU-P, was $75.90 the whole time. Same name, almost twice the price, different product. Our post on [power supplies](blog-power-supply-not-the-place-to-save.html) has the whole story.

For a complete PC, apply this to every line of the spec sheet. "16GB RAM (memory)" does not say one stick or two, or what speed; "1TB SSD (solid state drive)" does not name the drive. If a listing gives no model number for a part, ask, or assume it is the cheapest one that fits.

## 2. The real recent price, not the "was" price

A discount is measured from a starting price, and the seller picks the starting price. This fall that matters more than usual, because graphics card and memory prices have moved a lot.

| Part in our builds | Our log, Oct 14 | Our log, Nov 16 |
|---|---|---|
| Sapphire PULSE Radeon RX 9060 XT 16GB | $519.99 | $749.99 |
| Corsair Vengeance 32GB (2x16GB) DDR5-6000 CL36 | $479.99 | $499.99 |

Same card, same maker, $230 more in five weeks. So "15% off" a graphics card on Black Friday can still be well above what the same card cost a month earlier. Before you buy, look up the exact part number on a price history site, or check our [price report](price-report.html) for the parts we track. If the "deal" price is just back to where it was in October, that is fine - but know that is what you are getting.

Watch for promo-code prices too. In our log on Nov 8, a price comparison site listed the TeamGroup T-Force Delta RGB 16GB DDR5-6000 kit at $249.99 - but only after a $30 code. The price at the register without the code was $279.99. We log the sticker price, and you should compare sticker to sticker.

## 3. Who is actually selling it

On big marketplaces, the product page and the seller are two different things. "Sold and shipped by" the retailer itself is not the same as a third-party seller on the same page, and returns can differ too.

Unknown sellers show up at both ends of the price range. In our log, an unfamiliar store listed the RTX 5070 Ti we track at $2,087.95 on Nov 16, while Amazon, B&H, Best Buy and Newegg all had it at $1,499.99. A price far below everyone else from a seller you have never heard of deserves even more caution.

## 4. The return window, in writing

Read the return policy before you pay, not after. Things to look for: how many days, whether opened items come back, whether there is a restocking fee, and whether the item is "final sale" or "as is."

The California Attorney General's consumer page on refund policies is a good primer. It notes that some stores charge a restocking fee on certain products, that "final sale" and "as is" items may not be returnable, and that stores which clearly post a limited or no-refund policy are not required to give refunds. If a store fails to post such a policy as required, you can return the item with proof of purchase for a full refund within 30 days. Custom orders are not covered by that rule.

Keep the box and accessories until the window closes, and test a new PC or part as soon as it arrives.

## 5. Who handles the warranty

Every part has a maker's warranty - usually 1 to 5 years, with graphics cards often 2 to 3 and power supplies up to 10, as our [warranty page](warranty.html) explains. The question is what happens when something fails: with a prebuilt, check who you contact and whether the whole PC has to be shipped back.
