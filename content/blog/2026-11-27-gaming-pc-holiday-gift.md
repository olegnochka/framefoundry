Short answer: a gaming PC can be a great holiday gift, but it is not a sweater - plan it a little. Find out what they will actually play, decide whether they need a monitor and accessories too, leave enough time for the build, and think about who signs in to the accounts on day one. Our builds usually take 5 to 10 days from deposit to delivery, so ordering in the first half of December gives you room for a part that runs late. Here is the rest of the checklist.

## 1. Find out what they want to play

The games decide the build, so this is the one question worth asking, even if it gives away the surprise a little. Someone who plays Fortnite, Minecraft, Roblox or Valorant on a 1080p screen needs a very different PC from someone who wants big single-player games at 1440p or 4K, or who also edits video.

## 2. Check what else they need

A PC on its own does not get anyone playing. Make sure they have, or that you are also giving:

- A monitor - and match it to the build. Our post on [1080p, 1440p or 4K](blog-monitor-resolution-1080p-1440p-4k.html) explains which goes with which
- A keyboard and mouse
- A headset, especially if they play with friends online
- A desk with space for it, and a power outlet nearby

We sell monitors, keyboards, mice and headsets at the same cost plus 5% as the PC parts, and we set them up with the PC.

## 3. Leave enough time

Every PC is ordered new after your deposit - nothing sits on a shelf. Most are ready within 5 to 10 days of the deposit, depending on parts delivery. We give you an estimated date with the order and tell you right away if anything moves it, but dates are estimates rather than guarantees.

So for a gift, work backwards from the day you need it. The quote form has a "Needed by" field - put the holiday or birthday there. A quote is valid for 72 hours, because part prices change daily. If you buy one of our three current builds through checkout, the price is locked once you pay.

## 5. Decide who sets it up

This is the part people forget. A new PC needs Wi-Fi, a Windows account, updates and game launchers like Steam or Epic, and those launchers need the player's own accounts and passwords. Games, launchers and accounts belong to the owner - we install them only with permission and never keep passwords.

You have two easy options:

- Set it up together on the day. Plug it in, sign in, and let the updates run while everyone has breakfast
- Book in-home setup. Standard setup ($149) covers unboxing, connecting the monitor and accessories, the network, the Windows account, updates and launchers, plus a short walkthrough. Premium setup ($249) adds desk cable management, monitor settings like refresh rate, and a 30-day check-in

If the PC is for a child, premium setup also includes parental controls and screen-time limits set up with you. Our [parental controls checklist](blog-parental-controls-first-gaming-pc.html) covers what to switch on either way.

## 6. Know what happens if something goes wrong

Every PC we build comes with a 1-year labor warranty: if it stops working because of how we assembled it, or a part fails, we do the repair at your home within our service area and handle the manufacturer claim. Each part also keeps its own manufacturer warranty. Our [warranty page](warranty.html) has the details.

If you change your mind before we order the parts, you get a full refund of your deposit. After that, the [terms of sale](terms.html) explain how returns and restocking fees work.

## The short version

Ask about the games, budget for the screen and accessories, order with a week or two to spare, and plan who signs in. Do those four things and the gift is ready to play the day it is opened.
