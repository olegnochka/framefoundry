Short answer: buy a 1440p monitor unless you have a clear reason not to. A 1080p monitor is the right pick on a tight budget or for competitive shooters where frame rate is everything; 4K is for a $4,000-class PC, or for someone who also edits video or works on the same screen all day. Match it to the graphics card: our [Mainstream build](builds.html) is a 1080p-first PC that handles 1440p in popular games, the Performance build is made for 1440p, and the Quiet Creator is the one we point at 4K. Here is why, with the numbers.

## What resolution actually costs

A monitor's resolution is how many pixels the graphics card has to draw every frame:

| Name | Pixels | Per frame | Compared to 1080p |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1080p (Full HD) | 1920 x 1080 | about 2.1 million | 1x |
| 1440p (QHD) | 2560 x 1440 | about 3.7 million | 1.8x |
| 4K (UHD) | 3840 x 2160 | about 8.3 million | 4x |

That last column is the whole story. The same game at the same settings asks the card for nearly twice the work at 1440p and four times the work at 4K. The processor, memory and drive barely notice, so the monitor you choose is mostly a decision about which graphics card you need.

Upscaling softens this. AMD FSR (FidelityFX Super Resolution) and NVIDIA DLSS (Deep Learning Super Sampling) draw the game at a lower resolution and scale it up to the screen, which is how a card that struggles at native 4K can still look good on a 4K monitor. It is a safety net, not a plan: it only helps in games that support it.

## What most people play on

Valve's Steam Hardware Survey for September 2026 lists the primary display resolution of Steam users:

| Resolution | Share of Steam users |
|---|---|
| 1920 x 1080 | 47.9% |
| 2560 x 1440 | 27.0% (up 5.2 points in the month) |
| 2560 x 1600 | 5.2% |
| 3840 x 2160 | 4.8% |

Nearly half of Steam players are still on 1080p, 1440p is the fastest-growing group, and 4K is under one in twenty.

## 1080p: frame rate first

A 1080p monitor asks the least of the card, so the same card gives the highest frame rates there. For Fortnite, Valorant, Rocket League and Marvel Rivals, a 1080p monitor with a high refresh rate (144 Hz or more) is a sensible choice at any budget, because smoothness and reaction time matter more than detail.

The card in our Mainstream build is the Sapphire PULSE Radeon RX 9060 XT 16GB. AMD's own product page shows its headline figures at 1440p, which is why we describe the build as smooth 1080p at high settings and comfortable 1440p in today's popular games. On a 1080p screen it has room to spare. Keep 1080p to 24 or 27 inches; bigger than that and the pixels start to show.

## 1440p: the sweet spot

A 27-inch 1440p monitor is what we would put on most desks. Detail is clearly sharper than 1080p at that size, and the card does less than half the work of 4K.

At 1440p it runs with headroom, which is what you want for high frame rates in shooters and high settings in big single-player games. That build is $2,599 today, priced on Oct 27, 2026.

## 4K: when it is worth it

Four times the pixels of 1080p is a lot to ask, and this is where upscaling stops being optional in new games. 4K makes sense when the PC is also for work (video editing, photo work, a day of documents), when the screen is big (a 32-inch monitor or a living room TV, where 1440p looks coarse), and when the graphics card is top tier.

Our Quiet Creator build is the one we aim at 4K. Its Gigabyte GAMING OC GeForce RTX 5070 Ti 16GB has 16GB of GDDR7 memory on a 256-bit bus, and NVIDIA's spec page lists three DisplayPort and one HDMI output with a maximum of 4K at 480 Hz (with display stream compression). With the Ryzen 7 9800X3D, 32GB of memory and a 2TB drive, it is $3,999 today.

## Check the cable and the refresh rate

1. **Use DisplayPort or HDMI 2.1 for a high-refresh monitor.** All three of our cards have DisplayPort 2.1 and HDMI 2.1 outputs. An older HDMI cable or port may not carry the full refresh rate.
2. **Set the refresh rate in Windows.** A new monitor often runs at 60 Hz until you choose the higher rate in the display settings. Tuning refresh rate and color is part of our $249 premium [in-home setup](services.html); both setups connect the monitor and check it.
