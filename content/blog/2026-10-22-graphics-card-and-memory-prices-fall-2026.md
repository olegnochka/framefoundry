If you have been pricing a gaming PC for a few months, you are not imagining it: the two parts that matter most for gaming - the graphics card and memory - cost noticeably more this fall than they did in summer. Here is what the numbers show, which parts have stayed put, and how it affects a build you order today.

## What the numbers show

PCPartPicker publishes daily price-trend charts that average the prices of every listing in a category. As of October 21, 2026, their averages look like this (read off the charts, rounded):

| Part | Average in July | Average now | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radeon RX 9060 XT | about $520 | about $780 | +50% |
| Radeon RX 9070 XT | about $860 | about $1,130 | +31% |
| GeForce RTX 5070 Ti | about $1,100 | about $1,450 | +32% |
| DDR5-6000 32GB (2x16GB) | about $550 | about $630 | +15% |

Memory is the longer story. The same chart shows a 32GB DDR5-6000 kit averaging around $120 in mid-2025, so it has roughly quintupled in about a year. 1TB and 2TB NVMe SSDs more than doubled over the winter and have been flat since spring.

## What has not moved

Not everything is going up. On the same charts, Ryzen 5 and Ryzen 7 processors, and 850W-class 80+ Gold power supplies, have held roughly level all year. Cases and CPU coolers in our own daily price log have barely moved either.

The charts show what prices did, not why. We do not try to guess where they go next - nobody reliably can.

## Why an average is not your price

An average includes every listing, from the cheapest model to premium versions and third-party sellers asking far more. A single card you can actually buy is often well below the average. The cards in our builds today:

| Our build | Graphics card | Price today |
|---|---|---|
| Mainstream | Sapphire PULSE Radeon RX 9060 XT 16GB | $519.99 |
| Performance | ASRock Challenger Radeon RX 9070 XT 16GB | $879.99 |
| Quiet Creator | MSI SHADOW 3X OC GeForce RTX 5070 Ti 16GB | $1,229.99 |

Each of those is $220 to $260 under its chart average, because we track specific, well-reviewed models and check their price at Amazon, Newegg and other stores every morning.

## What it means if you are buying

- **Compare exact models, not "an RX 9070 XT".** Two cards with the same chip can be hundreds of dollars apart. Our [prebuilt vs custom guide](prebuilt-vs-custom-pc.html) has a checklist.
- **Memory is where costs hide.** With 32GB kits this expensive, our entry build starts at 16GB, which plays today's games fine; 32GB is the first upgrade we suggest.
- **Upgrade the right part.** If your processor is fine and only the graphics card is holding you back, a card swap can beat a whole new PC. See [upgrades](services.html).
- **Lock your price.** When you order a build, the price you pay is locked - if a part rises before we buy it, we absorb it. If the price we actually pay comes in lower, the difference comes off your balance.
