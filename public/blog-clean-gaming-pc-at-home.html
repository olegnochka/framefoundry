How to clean a gaming PC safely at home (and when to call us)

A safe way to clean dust out of a gaming PC at home - filters, fans, coolers and the power supply - plus the jobs worth leaving to a $79 tune-up.

5 min read cleaning maintenance tune-up

Short answer: yes, you can clean the dust out of a gaming PC yourself, and it is one of the best free things you can do for it. Power it down, take it somewhere you do not mind dust, clean the filters first, then work from the top of the case down with a soft brush and short bursts of compressed air - holding each fan still while you blow on it. Opening the case to clean dust does not affect our warranty. What we suggest leaving to us is the part after the dusting: fresh thermal paste on the processor, the fan curves and the checks that show whether it worked. Here is the step-by-step.

How often

Corsair's own cleaning guide suggests every three to six months as a general rule, every two to three months with pets, smoking or a dusty room, and more often for heavy use like gaming. With pets, open windows and carpet, many Valley homes sit at the shorter end. A flashlight tells you: grey fuzz on the front intake or the cooler fins means it is time.

What you need

  • Compressed air (a can or an electric duster)
  • A soft, clean brush, like a new paintbrush or a makeup brush
  • A microfiber cloth
  • A screwdriver for the side panel, if your case uses screws
  • Good light, ideally outside or in a garage

Skip the household vacuum inside the case. A brush and short bursts of air do the job without anything rigid getting near the parts.

Step by step

  1. Shut it down and cut the power. Shut down from Windows, flip the switch on the back of the power supply to off, and unplug the cable. Press the power button once afterwards to drain what is left.
  2. Move it. Carry the PC to where the dust can go. Hold it by the case, not by a glass panel.
  3. Clean the filters first. Most cases have dust filters on the front, top or bottom; many come off by hand or are held by magnets. Corsair's guide says they can be cleaned with a damp cloth or running water - just let them dry completely before they go back in.
  4. Open the side panel. Before touching anything inside, touch a bare metal part of the case to discharge static.
  5. Work from the top down. Brush the dust loose from fans and heatsink fins, then blow it out with short bursts of air.
  6. Hold every fan still. Put a finger or a plastic tool on the blades before you aim air at a fan. A fan spun by compressed air can go far faster than it was built to, which is hard on its bearing. This applies to case fans, the processor cooler and the graphics card.
  7. Be gentle around the motherboard and memory. Use short bursts of air only. Corsair's guide advises against brushing these parts.
  8. The power supply. Blow dust out through its grilles from the outside. Never open a power supply - it is not a user-serviceable part.
  9. Close it up and test. Put the filters back, plug everything in, switch the power supply back on and start a game you know well. It should sound calmer than before.

Hold the can of air upright while you use it - tilted cans can spit cold liquid propellant.

What cleaning will not fix

Dust is only half of why a PC gets loud and hot. The other half is thermal paste: the thin layer between the processor and its cooler dries out over a few years, and no amount of dusting reaches it. Replacing it means taking the cooler off, cleaning both surfaces and applying fresh paste - the step most home cleanings skip, and often the one that matters most on an older PC. Our post on why a PC gets loud and hot after a year or two covers both causes in more detail.

Fan curves are the same story: cooler parts let the fans run slower, but only if someone sets them that way - see what makes a gaming PC quiet.
