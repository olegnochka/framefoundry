Buyer's guide

Prebuilt vs custom gaming PC

How to compare gaming PC prices part by part, and when prebuilt, custom or do-it-yourself is the better deal.

There are three ways to get a gaming PC: buy a prebuilt from a brand or a store, have one built for you, or build it yourself. None is always the cheapest. The way to compare them is the same every time: look at the exact parts, then at what you get on top of them.

Compare the whole parts list, not just two parts

Most listings lead with the processor and the graphics card. Those matter most for games, but the parts that decide how long a PC lasts and how quiet it is - the power supply, motherboard, memory, SSD and cooling - are often listed vaguely or not at all. Before comparing prices, make sure you know the exact model of:

  • Processor (CPU) and graphics card (GPU)
  • Memory: size, speed and number of sticks
  • SSD: capacity and model (not just "1TB SSD")
  • Power supply: brand, wattage and efficiency rating
  • Motherboard, case and CPU cooler

When each option makes sense

Prebuilt

Good when a sale brings the price well below the parts cost, or when you want one box from one store. Check the full parts list and how warranty repairs work - often the whole PC has to be shipped back.

Custom-built for you

Good when you want specific parts, a quiet or compact build, every price shown, and someone local to deliver it, set it up and fix it if something goes wrong.

Build it yourself

Good if you enjoy it and have the time. You save the assembly fee and take on part compatibility, the build, BIOS and Windows setup, testing, and warranty claims yourself.

Common questions

Is it cheaper to buy a prebuilt gaming PC?

Sometimes, especially during big sales - and if a prebuilt is the better deal for you, we will say so. Compare the full parts list, not just the processor and graphics card, before deciding.

Is building your own PC cheaper?

On parts alone, yes - you skip the assembly fee. You also take on choosing compatible parts, the build itself, BIOS and driver setup, testing, and handling any warranty claim yourself.

What should a prebuilt spec sheet list?

The exact model of every part: processor, graphics card, motherboard, memory (size and speed), SSD, power supply (wattage and rating), case and cooler. Vague entries such as an unnamed power supply or 'RGB memory' are where costs are usually cut.

What warranty does a custom PC get?

Each part keeps its manufacturer's warranty. Ours adds one year of local labor: if something fails, we diagnose it and swap the part at your home. See the warranty.
