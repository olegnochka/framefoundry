Labor warranty vs manufacturer warranty: what each one covers

A part maker's warranty replaces the failed part. A local labor warranty covers finding it, swapping it and the claim itself. Here is how the two fit together.

5 min read warranty support buying

Short answer: a manufacturer warranty covers the part, and a labor warranty covers everything around it. When a graphics card or processor fails, its maker will usually repair or replace the part itself - but someone still has to work out which part failed, take it out, deal with the claim, ship it, put the replacement in and test the PC again. On every PC we build, our 1-year local labor warranty covers that work at your home, and each part keeps its own manufacturer warranty on top. Here is what each one actually does.

What a manufacturer warranty covers

Every part in a PC is a separate product from a separate company, and each comes with its own warranty. Our warranty page puts the usual range at 1 to 5 years, with graphics cards often 2 to 3 years and power supplies up to 10. These warranties protect you against a defective part. What they do not do is look after the PC.

AMD's boxed processor warranty is a good example of how these read. It covers a Ryzen processor (like the Ryzen 5 9600X in our Mainstream and Performance builds) for three years from the date it was purchased, and AMD will repair it, replace it, or refund its value. But the same page says plainly that it "does not cover any costs relating to removal or replacement" of the processor. It also does not cover damage from improper installation, and it tells you to contact your system builder first while the system warranty runs - AMD only takes claims directly after that.

Graphics cards work much the same way. Sapphire, who makes the PULSE Radeon RX 9060 XT in our Mainstream build, says on its customer service page that warranty questions go to the place you bought the card, that its warranty does not cover cards the user has taken apart, and that a replacement may be new, reworked or refurbished.

So a manufacturer warranty, on its own, means:

  • You figure out which part failed
  • You take the PC apart and remove that part
  • You find the receipt and serial number, open a claim, and pack and ship the part
  • You wait, then install whatever comes back and test it
  • Removal, installation and shipping costs are generally yours

What our 1-year labor warranty covers

For one year from delivery, if a PC we built stops working because of how we assembled it, or a part fails and needs to be swapped, we do that work for free at your home within our service area. The warranty lists exactly what is included:

  1. The trip to you
  2. The diagnosis - finding which part is actually at fault
  3. Removing the failed part
  4. Installing the replacement
  5. Re-testing the PC

During that same year, we also handle the manufacturer claim for you, including shipping the part. You do not need to find receipts or talk to five different support desks - you email us. We reply within one business day and try remote troubleshooting first, and if it is hardware, we book a visit. Our support page sums it up: if it is hardware, it is covered for a year and we come to you.

One more thing that happens before you ever see the PC: if a part is dead on arrival or fails during our testing, we replace it before delivery at no cost to you.

What neither one covers

Both kinds of warranty are about defects, not accidents. Ours does not cover drops, liquid, power surges, pets, or opening the PC and changing parts yourself - although opening it to clean dust is fine, as our post on cleaning a gaming PC at home explains. Software problems, viruses, lost data and game or account issues are not covered either (we can help with those as a paid service), and neither is normal wear like fan noise growing over the years. Overclocking beyond the settings we delivered is out too.

After the first year

Your parts stay covered by their makers for however long their warranties run, and you can still file those claims yourself. After our first year, we can still help with claims for a labor fee quoted up front.

It also includes one $79 tune-up each plan year. The same exclusions apply, and the replacement parts themselves still come through the manufacturers' warranties. The details are on our services page.

Your rights in California

Our warranty is a promise on top of the law, not instead of it. It says so directly: you may also have rights under California law, including the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act, and federal law, and nothing in our warranty limits those.

The practical takeaway

When you compare a custom PC with one you build yourself or buy elsewhere, look at both warranties. Manufacturer warranties are similar wherever you buy the parts. The difference is who does the work when one fails - and whether that happens at your desk or in a shipping box.
