A parent's guide to a first gaming PC

What a first gaming PC needs, the extras people forget, and how to set up parental controls before the first login.

If your kid is asking for a gaming PC, the good news is that the games most kids play - Fortnite, Valorant, Minecraft, Roblox, Rocket League and Marvel Rivals - do not need a top-end machine. What they need is a reliable PC that runs those games smoothly, a decent monitor, and parental controls set up before the first login. This guide covers what to buy, what people forget, and how to set it up.

What a first gaming PC actually needs

  • A current graphics card with enough memory. This decides how games look and run. Our entry build uses a Sapphire PULSE Radeon RX 9060 XT 16GB.
  • A modern 6-core processor such as the AMD Ryzen 5 9600X.
  • 16GB of memory is enough for these games; 32GB is a sensible upgrade later.
  • A 1TB SSD (solid state drive). Games are large; 1TB holds several at once and loads them quickly.
  • Wi-Fi built in, in case the router is in another room - though a cable is better for online games when you can run one.

That is exactly our Mainstream build, at $2,099 before sales tax.

The extras people forget

  • A monitor. A 1080p or 1440p gaming monitor with a high refresh rate makes a bigger difference than most upgrades inside the PC.
  • Keyboard, mouse and a headset with a microphone for talking to friends.
  • A spot with airflow. Not inside a closed cabinet; leave space behind and on the sides.
  • Accounts. A Microsoft account set up as a child account, plus accounts for the launchers and games they play.

We sell monitors, keyboards and headsets at the same cost plus 5% as everything else and set them up with the PC.

Parental controls and screen time

Set these up on day one - it is much harder to add limits later.

  1. Windows: create a Microsoft family group and add your child's account. Microsoft Family Safety handles screen-time limits, age ratings for apps and games, content filters and approval before anything is bought.
  2. Game launchers: Steam, Epic Games and the Xbox app each have their own family settings - turn them on and set a PIN.
  3. In each game: many games, Roblox and Fortnite included, have their own parental controls for chat and spending.

Common questions

What gaming PC does a kid need for Fortnite, Roblox and Minecraft?

Not the most expensive one. Our Mainstream build ($2,099) runs Fortnite, Valorant, Minecraft, Roblox, Rocket League and Marvel Rivals smoothly at 1080p on high settings, which is what most first setups use.

Desktop or gaming laptop?

For a PC that stays on a desk, a desktop gives more performance for the money, runs cooler and quieter, and can be upgraded part by part later. A laptop makes sense only if it really needs to travel.

How do I set parental controls on a gaming PC?

Use a Microsoft family group (Microsoft Family Safety) for Windows screen time, app and game age limits and purchase approval, then turn on the family settings in each game launcher (Steam, Epic Games, Xbox app) and in the games themselves. Our premium in-home setup sets these up with you.

Can the PC be upgraded as they get older?

Yes. A desktop like this has room for more memory, a bigger drive or a faster graphics card later. We do upgrades at your home and quote the work before we start.
